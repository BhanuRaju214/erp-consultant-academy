# Video script: Project to Profit, explained

Animated explainer for the Project to Profit business process (video /videos/project-to-profit-explained.mp4 in the portal). Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Project to Profit**. How a signed contract becomes delivered work, billed revenue and a known margin. | Welcome. In this video you will learn the Project to Profit process: how a signed contract becomes a project, how hours and costs are captured, how the customer is billed, and how Odoo shows the real margin of every project. |
| 2 | **What is Project to Profit?**. Cards: Link everything · Bill by contract · See the margin | Project to Profit is the revenue cycle of services, design and contracting businesses. The goal is to deliver projects within scope, time and budget, while billing everything that is billable. Every hour, expense and purchase is linked to the project, invoices follow the contract model, and revenue is compared with cost in real time. |
| 3 | **Who takes part?**. Cards: Account Manager · Project Manager · Team Member · Purchasing Officer · Billing Accountant · Finance Controller · Client | Many people take part. The account manager agrees the scope and pricing with the client. The project manager plans tasks and approves timesheets. Team members log their hours, purchasing buys materials for the project, the billing accountant raises the invoices, and the finance controller reviews profitability. |
| 4 | **Six steps from contract to profit**. Animated flow: Project from contract ▸ Tasks & milestones ▸ Timesheets ▸ Costs ▸ Bill the client ▸ Profitability | Here is the big picture: six steps. Create the project from the contract, plan tasks and milestones, record timesheets, capture material and service costs, bill the customer, and analyse profitability. The project's analytic account collects every revenue and every cost, so the margin is always visible. |
| 5 | Step 1: **Create the project from the contract** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Sales Order S00452** card | Step one. Lumen and Loom Interiors signs an office fit-out with Harbourline Logistics: a fixed design fee of twelve thousand in two milestones, site supervision at ninety an hour, and furniture at cost plus twenty percent. Confirming the sales order creates the project and its analytic account automatically. No journal entry yet. |
| 6 | Step 2: **Plan tasks and milestones** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Project HL-2025-07** card | Step two. The project manager, Ananya, breaks the scope into tasks with owners, deadlines and allocated hours: space planning, concept, detailed drawings, furniture procurement and site supervision. Two billing milestones are defined, concept approved and drawings handed over, so invoicing follows delivery. |
| 7 | Step 3: **Record and approve timesheets** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Timesheets** card | Step three. Each team member logs hours against tasks. The designers log one hundred and sixty hours at a cost of forty an hour, and the supervisor sixty hours at forty five. Timesheets create analytic cost lines only, not general ledger entries, because the wages themselves are booked through payroll. |
| 8 | Step 4: **Capture material and service costs** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Project costs** card | Step four. Materials and subcontractors are bought for the project, each carrying the project's analytic account. The purchasing officer orders furniture from Crestwood for eighteen thousand, delivered directly to the client. A painting subcontractor bills four thousand, coded to the project but not re-billable, because it is included in the design fee. |
| 9 | Step 5: **Bill the customer** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Invoices to Harbourline** card | Step five. Invoices follow the contract. When the concept is approved, the first milestone of six thousand is invoiced, and the second when the drawings are handed over. The furniture is re-invoiced at cost plus twenty percent, twenty one thousand six hundred, and sixty validated supervision hours bring five thousand four hundred. Total revenue: thirty nine thousand. |
| 10 | Step 6: **Analyse project profitability** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Profitability · HL-2025-07** card | Step six. Revenue is compared with labour, materials and subcontracting. The project earns thirty nine thousand against costs of thirty one thousand one hundred, a margin of seven thousand nine hundred, or twenty point three percent. Design earned only thirteen percent after three rounds of concept changes, so next time the fixed fee covers two revisions only. |
| 11 | **The entries behind a project**: 1. Subcontractor bill · 2. Milestone invoice · 3. Furniture re-invoiced | Let's follow the money. Timesheets post nothing to the general ledger; they only add analytic cost to the project. A subcontractor bill debits subcontracting four thousand plus input tax. A milestone invoice credits service revenue six thousand plus tax. And re-invoiced furniture credits revenue twenty one thousand six hundred, and every line carries the project's analytic account, which makes the profitability report possible. |
| 12 | **Common risks, and how Odoo controls them**. Cards: Unlogged or late timesheets · Scope creep · Costs not coded to the project · Re-billable costs forgotten | What can go wrong? Unlogged timesheets: send reminders and validate weekly. Scope creep: handle every change as a change request with new order lines. Costs not coded to the project: make the analytic distribution mandatory. And forgotten re-billable costs: use the re-invoice expenses setting and review the sales order before each billing run. |
| 13 | **Every hour and cost, one margin**. Animated flow: Contract ▸ Tasks ▸ Timesheets ▸ Costs ▸ Invoices ▸ Margin | To recap: contract, tasks, timesheets, costs, invoices and margin. Every hour and every cost lands on the project. Next, open the Odoo Sales and Odoo Accounting courses to practise service invoicing and analytic reporting with the click-through simulators. |
