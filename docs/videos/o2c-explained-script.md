# Video script: Order to Cash, explained

Animated explainer for the Order to Cash business process (video /videos/o2c-explained.mp4 in the portal). Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Order to Cash**. How a customer's request becomes cash in the bank, and how Odoo runs every step. | Welcome. In this video you will learn the Order to Cash process: how a customer's request becomes cash in the bank, and how Odoo supports every single step. |
| 2 | **What is Order to Cash?**. Cards: Capture demand · Deliver correctly · Collect cash | Order to Cash, or O2C, is the complete revenue cycle. It starts with the first contact with a prospect and ends when the customer's payment is reconciled in the bank. The goal is simple: capture demand, deliver the right goods at the right price, and collect the cash quickly and accurately. |
| 3 | **Who takes part?**. Cards: Sales Representative · Sales Manager · Credit Controller · Warehouse · Accounts Receivable · Treasury · Customer · Management | Many people take part. Sales representatives qualify leads and prepare quotations. The sales manager approves discounts. The credit controller sets credit limits. The warehouse picks and ships the goods. Accounts receivable issues the invoices, and treasury records the payments and reconciles the bank. And of course, the customer. |
| 4 | **Nine steps from lead to cash**. Animated flow: Capture lead ▸ Qualify ▸ Customer master ▸ Quotation ▸ Sales order ▸ Deliver ▸ Invoice ▸ Payment ▸ Reconcile | Here is the big picture: nine steps. Capture the lead, qualify it, set up the customer, send a quotation, confirm the sales order, deliver the goods, invoice, receive the payment, and finally reconcile the bank. In Odoo these steps run across CRM, Contacts, Sales, Inventory and Accounting, all in one database, so every document is linked to the next. |
| 5 | Step 1–2: **Capture and qualify the lead** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Opportunity** card | Step one and two. A prospect shows interest, through the website, an email or a phone call. Odoo records it as a lead. The salesperson checks budget, authority, need and timing. If it is real, the lead becomes an opportunity with an expected revenue and probability. There is no accounting impact yet: it only feeds the sales forecast. |
| 6 | Step 3: **Set up the customer master** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Customer** card | Step three. Before quoting, the customer must exist as a clean master record: legal name, addresses, tax I D, payment terms, pricelist and fiscal position. It creates no journal entry, but every later document copies these values. Clean customer data prevents wrong taxes, wrong prices and wrong deliveries. |
| 7 | Step 4: **Prepare and send the quotation** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Quotation** card | Step four: the quotation. The salesperson lists the products, quantities, prices, discounts and taxes, and sends it to the customer as a P D F or an online portal link. Discounts above policy need the manager's approval. A quotation has no accounting impact, and it does not reserve stock. |
| 8 | Step 5: **Confirm the sales order** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Sales Order S00045** card | Step five. When the customer accepts, the quotation is confirmed into a sales order. This is the binding commitment. In Odoo it automatically creates the delivery order and reserves the stock. Credit checks happen here for credit controlled customers. Still no journal entry. |
| 9 | Step 6: **Pick, pack and deliver** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Delivery WH/OUT/00031** card | Step six. The warehouse picks, packs and ships the goods. In one step delivery it is a single transfer; in two or three steps there are separate pick, pack and ship operations. Whatever is short becomes a backorder. Validating the delivery reduces stock on hand, and with perpetual valuation it posts the stock entry. |
| 10 | Step 7: **Issue the customer invoice** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Invoice INV/2026/0045** card | Step seven: the invoice. Finance invoices according to the invoicing policy, usually delivered quantities, so the customer is never billed for goods not shipped. When the invoice is posted, revenue is recognised: Odoo debits accounts receivable and credits sales and tax payable. |
| 11 | Step 8–9: **Receive the payment, reconcile the bank** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Invoice INV/2026/0045** card | Steps eight and nine. The customer pays, and the payment is registered against the open invoice. The invoice shows In Payment. When the bank statement arrives, each line is matched with the payment. Only then is the invoice Paid, and the cycle is closed. |
| 12 | **The journal entries behind O2C**: 1. Invoice posted · 2. Payment registered · 3. Bank reconciled | Let's follow the money. When the invoice is posted, receivable is debited eleven thousand five hundred, and sales and tax are credited. With perpetual valuation the cost of goods sold is booked too. The payment moves the amount from receivable to outstanding receipts. And the bank reconciliation finally moves it into the bank account. Remember: revenue is recognised at the invoice, not at the order. |
| 13 | **Common risks, and how Odoo controls them**. Cards: Customer over credit limit · Not enough stock · Wrong price or discount · Billing goods not shipped | What can go wrong? A customer over the credit limit: Odoo warns on the order. Not enough stock: check the forecast on the quotation and use replenishment rules. Wrong prices: keep pricelists central and restrict who can change them. And billing for goods not shipped: invoice on delivered quantities. |
| 14 | **One process, one database**. Animated flow: Lead ▸ Opportunity ▸ Customer ▸ Quotation ▸ Sales order ▸ Delivery ▸ Invoice ▸ Payment ▸ Bank | To recap: lead, opportunity, customer, quotation, sales order, delivery, invoice, payment and bank. One process, one database, every document linked. Next, open the Odoo Sales course and try the quotation to invoice simulator yourself. |
