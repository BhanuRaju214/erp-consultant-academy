# Video script: Phase 8 — Go-Live

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-8-go-live.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 8 — Go-Live**. Not a single click: a rehearsed cutover with owners, timings and checkpoints. | Welcome to lesson nine of Implementation Methodology. In this video you will learn how a go-live really works: the cutover plan, readiness criteria, the go or no-go decision, the rollback plan, and hypercare. |
| 2 | Step 40: **A calm Monday** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Cutover plan** card | Bluewater Marine Supplies, a fictional company, went live on the first working day of a new month. On Friday evening the old system was frozen. Over the weekend the team loaded final data and reconciled balances, and on Monday forty users logged in and started trading. Behind that calm Monday sat a detailed cutover plan, rehearsed twice. |
| 3 | **The language of go-live**. Cards: Cutover · Freeze · Go / no-go · Rollback plan · Hypercare | Cutover is the period and set of activities that move the business from the old system to the new one. The freeze is the point after which no new transactions are entered in the old system. The go or no-go decision is formal and based on readiness criteria. The rollback plan says what happens if go-live is aborted, and hypercare is the intensive support period that follows. |
| 4 | Counting down to go-live | At T minus four weeks, the team confirms readiness and approves the cutover plan. At T minus two weeks comes the dress rehearsal, a timed final trial migration on staging. At T minus one week, the first go or no-go check. Two days out, master data is loaded and frozen. Then Friday's freeze, Saturday's load and reconciliation, Sunday's final decision, and Monday's go-live. |
| 5 | **The order matters**. Animated flow: Freeze old system ▸ Stock count ▸ Load stock & balances ▸ Reconcile ▸ Smoke test ▸ Final go/no-go ▸ Enable users | The cutover weekend follows a strict order. Freeze transactions first, so the numbers stop moving, and only then count stock. Load opening stock, open receivables and payables and the trial balance, then reconcile them with the source. Key users run a smoke test of the critical transactions. Only then does the sponsor decide, and users are enabled last. |
| 6 | Evidence, not optimism | The go or no-go decision rests on readiness criteria agreed in advance. User acceptance testing is signed with no open critical or high defects. The final migration has been rehearsed. Every user exists with the right roles and has been trained. Printers, scanners, email and backups are checked, the hypercare team is ready, and customers and vendors know about the freeze. |
| 7 | **Three possible outcomes**. Cards: GO · Conditional GO · NO-GO | The sponsor decides, on evidence prepared by the project managers. If everything passes, including reconciliation within an agreed tolerance such as half a percent, it is a go. If only high defects remain, each with a tested workaround the process owner accepts in writing, a conditional go is possible, but use it rarely. Otherwise it is a no-go, and the rollback plan keeps the old system running. |
| 8 | Step 70%: **Criteria, not dates** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Go/no-go record** card | In the lesson's role-play, one critical defect is open on Sunday: lot-tracked deliveries cannot be validated. The sponsor says, we go whatever happens. Bring the decision back to the criteria everyone signed. Quantify it: about seventy percent of deliveries cannot ship or be invoiced. Then recommend clearly: no-go today, retest the fix, reconvene Wednesday, keep the old system live, and inform customers. |
| 9 | Step 0.02%: **Rehearsal changed the plan** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Stock reconciliation** card | Bluewater's rehearsal showed the opening stock load took five hours instead of two, because of three thousand lot numbers. The plan was adjusted to load lots on Saturday morning. A rollback trigger was defined: if stock value differed by more than half a percent by Sunday afternoon, go-live would move one week. The actual difference was tiny, explained by rounding, and go-live proceeded. |
| 10 | **Stay until the business is stable**. Cards: Daily stand-up · Floor-walk · Watch health metrics · Exit on evidence | Hypercare lasts two to four weeks. Hold a daily stand-up, floor-walk in busy departments, and watch stuck documents and integration errors. Log every issue as a ticket, never fix data directly in the database, and never let transactions return to the old system. Exit hypercare when tickets fall to an agreed level and month end close succeeds, not on a fixed date. |
| 11 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Rehearsed cutover · Formal go/no-go · Freeze, count, reconcile · Rollback + hypercare | To sum up. Go-live is a rehearsed cutover with owners, timings and dependencies. Decide with readiness criteria and a formal go or no-go. Freeze, count, load and reconcile before enabling users, always have a rollback plan, and support the business through hypercare. Now try the exercises and the role-play in this lesson, starting with putting Bluewater's cutover weekend in order. |
