# Video script: Phase 1 — Business Discovery

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-1-business-discovery.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 1 — Business Discovery**. Finding what the client did not think to tell you. | Welcome to lesson two. In this video you will learn how to run business discovery, the first phase of an E R P project, and how to turn interviews and real documents into an as-is description the client confirms. |
| 2 | Step ?: **"We need Odoo for sales and stock"** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Original request** card | Crescent Tiles and Stone, a fictional importer, stocks tiles in two warehouses and sells to contractors and walk-in retail customers. The owner says, we need Odoo for sales and stock. In discovery, the consultant learns that tiles are quoted by square metre but sold by the box, that batches differ in shade so each customer must receive a single batch, and that contractors get credit while retail customers pay upfront. None of that was in the request. Discovery exists to find this. |
| 3 | **What discovery must achieve**. Cards: Industry · Operations · Pain points · Evidence · AS-IS document | Discovery has five objectives. Understand the industry and how this company makes money. Understand operations end to end, meaning who does what, with which documents and systems. Identify pain points and their business impact. Collect real evidence, such as forms, spreadsheets, reports and sample transactions. And finally, produce an as-is document that the client confirms is accurate. |
| 4 | **From preparation to a validated AS-IS**. Animated flow: Prepare ▸ Interview ▸ Observe & collect ▸ Document AS-IS ▸ Validate | Discovery runs in five steps. Prepare by researching the industry and sending a questionnaire a week ahead. Interview stakeholders. Sit with users while they work and collect every form, spreadsheet and report, then ask who uses it and what decision it supports, because many reports are produced but never read. Document the as-is with narratives, diagrams, volumes and systems. Then validate it, because the document is only finished once the client has corrected and confirmed it. |
| 5 | **Talk to each group separately**. Cards: Management · Process owners · Key users · IT | Interview four groups separately. Management explains strategy and success measures. Process owners know the rules, approvals and exceptions. Key users know the real daily workarounds and volumes, which managers often do not. And I T covers systems, integrations and security. Ask open questions, like walk me through what happens when a customer orders something you do not have in stock, and then probe the exceptions. |
| 6 | Step 15: **"Can Odoo just stop returns?"** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Pain point PP-02** card | In the lesson's role-play, the sales lead asks whether Odoo can just stop returns. Showing the returns screen would be a mistake, because returns are only a symptom. Instead, ask them to walk you through the last return. You learn shades did not match, so probe how the storekeeper picks boxes and whether the delivery note shows the batch. Quantify it: about fifteen returns a month, each costing two hours and a second truck. Finally, play it back and log it as pain point P P zero two. |
| 7 | Current systems & pain-point register | Every pain point goes into a register with the process, the system or tool, the impact in time, money or risk, how often it happens and who raised it. At Crescent, orders typed twice into a spreadsheet cost around six hours a day. Impact and frequency later justify priority and let you measure improvement after go-live. |
| 8 | Common discovery pitfalls | Watch for these pitfalls. Jumping to the solution, by demonstrating screens before you understand the business. Talking only to managers. Missing exceptions like returns, cancellations and partial deliveries. Ignoring volumes, since fifty orders a month and five thousand orders a day need very different designs. And accepting claims without evidence. If the owner says they have a weekly stock report, ask to see it, who reads it, and what decision it supports. |
| 9 | Step 2: **Two errors corrected — a healthy outcome** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **AS-IS document** card | At Crescent, discovery revealed three units of measure, square metre, box and pallet, lot tracking for shade batches, two customer payment policies and an import landed cost process. The as-is document captured all of this with sample documents attached. The client then corrected two errors in the draft. That is healthy: it proves the client read and owns the document. |
| 10 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Find the unspoken · Interview separately · Evidence beats assertions · Validated AS-IS | To sum up. Discovery finds the requirements the client did not mention. Interview management, process owners, key users and I T separately. Collect real documents and ask what decision each one supports. Record pain points with impact and frequency, and get the as-is document confirmed. Now try the exercises in this lesson, and practise the role-play on probing a pain point with Crescent's sales lead. |
