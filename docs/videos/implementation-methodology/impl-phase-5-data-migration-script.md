# Video script: Phase 5 — Data Migration

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-5-data-migration.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 5 — Data Migration**. Extract, cleanse, map, validate, load and reconcile, then rehearse it all again. | Welcome to lesson six of Implementation Methodology. In this video you will learn how to move a client's data into the new E R P safely, in the right order, with balances that match the old system to the cent. |
| 2 | Step 6,200: **Dirtier than anyone thinks** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Migration scope** card | Summit Hardware Supplies has six thousand two hundred products, eleven hundred customers and two hundred and forty vendors, spread across an old accounting package and spreadsheets. On go-live morning, sales must find every customer, the warehouse must see correct stock, and finance must start with matching balances. Migration is where many projects slip, because data is always dirtier than the client believes. |
| 3 | **Six steps, repeated in trial loads**. Animated flow: Extract ▸ Cleanse ▸ Map / transform ▸ Validate ▸ Load (trial) ▸ Reconcile | Every migration follows six steps. Extract full data from each source and record the row counts. Cleanse it, and note that cleansing is owned by the business, because only they know which records are real. Map source fields to target fields and translate old codes. Validate mandatory and unique values, then load with import templates, and reconcile counts and totals. You repeat this in two or three trial loads before the final one. |
| 4 | Masters before transactions | A record can only point to records that already exist. So configuration comes first, then product categories, customers and vendors. Products follow, because they need categories, units of measure, taxes and vendors. Then pricelists and bills of materials, opening stock, open documents, and finally opening balances. Load in the wrong order and the import fails with missing reference errors. |
| 5 | **Count every balance exactly once**. Cards: Opening stock · Open AR / AP · Trial balance · History | Opening stock is loaded per product and location, and per lot if tracked, at the cut-off date and agreed cost. Open receivables and payables are loaded invoice by invoice, so they can be collected, paid and aged correctly. The trial balance goes in as an opening journal entry, using a suspense account for the parts loaded separately, so nothing is counted twice. Old closed history usually stays in the read-only old system. |
| 6 | Every field has a rule and an owner | The mapping sheet is your contract for each object. For every target field it records the source column, the transformation rule, a validation rule, an example and an owner. Customer names are trimmed and put in title case, tax numbers lose their spaces, and old payment term codes such as N thirty are translated to thirty days. When a load fails, this sheet tells you exactly where to fix it. |
| 7 | Explain every difference | Reconciliation compares counts and totals between source and target. Summit loaded fewer customers than it extracted, but the gap is explained: twenty eight duplicates were merged. Seven hundred and twenty inactive products were deliberately excluded. Stock value and open receivables match exactly. Each line is signed by a business owner, because without that sign-off, disputes surface months later. |
| 8 | Step 900: **Rejected, fixed, rehearsed** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Product load** card | Summit's first trial load rejected nine hundred products, because category codes in the item master did not match the new category tree. The mapping sheet gained a translation table and the second load succeeded. A third, full rehearsal took six hours, and that timing went straight into the cutover plan. This is exactly why one trial load is never enough. |
| 9 | **Mistakes that cost go-live dates**. Cards: Cleansing too late · Only one trial load · Double-counted AR · Wrong load order · No sign-off · Junk history | The classic mistakes are predictable. Teams start cleansing too late, when it should begin during discovery. They run only one trial load. They double count receivables by loading them as invoices and inside the trial balance. And they skip reconciliation sign-off, or migrate years of junk history just in case. Remind the client early that the business owns data quality, while you own the method and tools. |
| 10 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Six steps, rehearsed · Business owns the data · Dependency order · Reconcile and sign | To sum up. Migration means extract, cleanse, map, validate, load and reconcile, repeated through trial loads. The business owns data quality. Respect dependency order, load open receivables and payables individually without double counting, and get signed reconciliation. Now try the exercises in this lesson, starting with putting Summit's data loads in the right dependency order. |
