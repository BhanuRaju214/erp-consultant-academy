# Video script: Phase 7 — Testing and Training

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-7-testing-training.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 7 — Testing and Training**. Testing proves the system works; training proves people can use it. | Welcome to lesson eight of Implementation Methodology. In this video you will learn the layers of testing, how to write test cases and manage defects, how user acceptance testing works, and how to train users so they are ready on day one. |
| 2 | Step 1 → 4 wk: **Testing found it first** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Test plan** card | Pinecrest Building Materials, a fictional company, planned one week for testing. On day two, users found that invoices for partial deliveries were wrong, a purchase approval blocked every order, and two warehouse users could not see delivery orders at all. Testing grew to four weeks. Painful, yes, but far cheaper than finding these problems after go-live. Testing and training together lead to business acceptance. |
| 3 | **Test in layers**. Animated flow: Unit ▸ Functional ▸ Integration ▸ UAT ▸ Sign-off | Testing happens in layers. Developers unit test each customization in isolation. Consultants run functional tests to check each feature works as designed, then integration tests of end-to-end flows with key users. Key users then perform user acceptance testing with real scenarios, and process owners sign off. Regression testing runs after every fix or deployment, to prove nothing nearby broke. |
| 4 | Traced, repeatable, with clear expected results | Every test case links to a requirement and can be run by someone who did not write it. It lists preconditions, test data, steps and the expected result. Test case ten checks that a quote in square metres becomes one hundred boxes on the delivery, and it passed. Test case twenty expected an order over the credit limit to be blocked, but it was confirmed, so it failed and defect seven was logged. |
| 5 | **Severity is about business impact**. Cards: Critical · High · Medium · Low | Each defect gets a severity based on business impact and workaround, not on how hard the fix is. Critical means the process has stopped, like warehouse users who cannot see any deliveries. High means a major function is wrong and the workaround is hard. Medium has a workaround, and low is cosmetic. Defects are also classified by root cause: configuration, data, code or training. After a fix, retest the case and run regression. |
| 6 | **The business proves it works**. Cards: Real scenarios, real data · Key users drive · End-to-end cycles · Exit criteria | User acceptance testing uses real business scenarios with trial migrated data, never demo data. Key users execute the tests, and consultants support without driving, because acceptance must come from the business. Cover full cycles, from quote to payment, from requisition to payment, and month end close. A typical exit criterion is that all critical and high cases pass, with no open critical or high defects. |
| 7 | **Train by role, hands-on, close to go-live**. Cards: Train-the-trainer · Role-based sessions · Hands-on · Role manuals | With train the trainer, consultants train key users, who then train end users. Sessions are role based, because a storekeeper does not need the accounting configuration. Training is hands-on in a training database with realistic data, supported by user manuals per role. And timing matters: train too early and users forget, too late and they have no time to practise. |
| 8 | Step 3 wk: **"Let's skip training"** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Training plan** card | Testing at Pinecrest ran three weeks late, and the sponsor asks to cancel end-user training. Simply agreeing is the poor answer, because untrained users create wrong data from day one. The best answer acknowledges the pressure, shows what untrained users would cost, and proposes a shorter plan. Key users train their own teams by role in half-day sessions, backed by hypercare floor-walkers after go-live. |
| 9 | Step 23: **Signed on real orders** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **UAT summary** card | Pinecrest's revised user acceptance testing used eighty five test cases traced to sixty two must and should requirements. Twenty three defects were logged: nine configuration, six data, five code and three training. Notice that most were not code at all. All critical and high defects were closed, and the warehouse manager signed after running a full day of real orders in staging. |
| 10 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Test in layers · Trace every case · Manage defects · Train by role | To sum up. Test in layers. Link every test case to a requirement with a clear expected result. Log, classify, fix, retest and regression test every defect. User acceptance testing is done by the business with realistic data and ends in formal acceptance. Now try the exercises and the role-play in this lesson, starting with matching Pinecrest's defects to their severity. |
