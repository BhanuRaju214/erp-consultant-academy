# Video script: Phase 9 — Post-Go-Live Support

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-9-post-go-live-support.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 9 — Post-Go-Live Support**. Go-live is the beginning of value, not the end of the project. | Welcome to lesson ten of Implementation Methodology. In this video you will learn how structured support protects the E R P investment after go-live: logging and prioritising tickets, finding root causes, fixing safely, and measuring adoption. |
| 2 | Step 3 mo: **Was the ERP worth it?** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Support today** card | Three months after go-live, Orchid Event Rentals, a fictional company, has a growing pile of emails titled urgent, users who have quietly gone back to spreadsheets for quotations, and a manager asking whether the E R P was worth it. Structured support prevents this. Issues are logged and solved systematically, knowledge is captured, and adoption is measured against the business case. |
| 3 | **One channel, every issue**. Animated flow: Logged ▸ Triaged ▸ Assigned ▸ Investigating ▸ Resolved ▸ User confirms ▸ Closed | Every issue goes through one channel, such as a helpdesk tool, a ticket portal or a dedicated email address linked to a ticket system. A ticket is logged, then triaged with a category and priority, assigned and investigated. Code defects are escalated to the developer or vendor. Once resolved, the user confirms the fix before the ticket is closed. |
| 4 | The category decides who resolves it | Categorising tells you who fixes the issue and how. A how-to question gets a knowledge base article. A configuration error goes to the consultant, and a data problem gets a correcting document. Customization defects go to the developer, standard product defects go to vendor support, and change requests go into the backlog. This also follows the escalation path: key users first, then consultants, then developers or vendor. |
| 5 | Not everything is urgent | Priority definitions stop every request being treated as urgent. Priority one means the business has stopped with no workaround, for example invoicing has stopped for all customers, so you respond within an hour and resolve the same day. Priority two is a major process impaired, priority three has a workaround, and priority four is cosmetic. These targets are examples; the real ones are agreed in the support contract. |
| 6 | Step P2: **SUP-0142: not enough stock** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **SUP-0142** card | Here is a well-documented ticket. A warehouse user cannot validate a delivery for a white chair item, because the system reports not enough stock. It is categorised as data, priority two. The root cause: opening stock was loaded to the wrong location. The fix was an internal transfer, verified with the user, plus a preventive action and a knowledge base article. Closed in under two hours. |
| 7 | **Ask why until you can fix it permanently**. Animated flow: No stock seen ▸ Wrong location ▸ Wrong column in file ▸ Free-text location ▸ No checklist step | Why could the delivery not be validated? The system saw no stock. Why? The stock was in a different location. Why? The opening stock file used the wrong location column. Why? The template allowed free text. Why? The migration checklist had no location check. So you move the stock, which is corrective, and fix the checklist, which is preventive, so the issue never returns. |
| 8 | **Fix safely, keep the audit trail**. Cards: Configuration · Data · Code | Fix safely. Test configuration fixes in staging first, update the configuration workbook, then apply them in production. Correct data with standard documents such as credit notes, inventory adjustments and reversals, because direct database edits break the audit trail. Escalate code defects with full reproduction steps. And avoid support by personal phone calls and chats, because nothing gets tracked. |
| 9 | Prove the value | Support is also about proving value. Track active users against licensed users, and look for documents created outside the E R P, because shadow spreadsheets grow silently. Watch tickets per week by category, and measure the business case K P Is, such as days sales outstanding falling from sixty two to forty eight days, or month end close within four days by month three. |
| 10 | Step 64%: **Tickets halved in six weeks** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Support report** card | Orchid introduced a ticket form and priority rules. In the first month, sixty four percent of tickets were how-to questions, so the consultant wrote fifteen knowledge base articles and ran two refresher sessions on quotations. Tickets fell by half in six weeks. And the quotation spreadsheets disappeared once root-cause analysis found and fixed a pricing configuration issue. |
| 11 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: One place, every issue · Find the root cause · Fix safely · Measure adoption | To sum up. Log every issue in one place with a category and priority. Diagnose training, configuration, data or code, and find the root cause. Fix safely, build a knowledge base, and monitor adoption and K P Is. Now try the exercises in this lesson, starting with categorising Orchid's tickets and choosing the right priority. |
