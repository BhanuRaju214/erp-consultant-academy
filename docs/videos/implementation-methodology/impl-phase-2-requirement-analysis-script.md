# Video script: Phase 2 — Requirement Analysis

Narrated lesson video for ERP Implementation Methodology. Video file: public/videos/impl-phase-2-requirement-analysis.mp4. Voice: en-IN-NeerjaNeural.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Phase 2 — Requirement Analysis**. Turning workshop notes into a numbered, classified, prioritised RTM. | Welcome to lesson three. In this video you will learn how to turn discovery notes into clear requirements, classify and prioritise them, and build the requirement traceability matrix that drives the rest of the project. |
| 2 | Step 40: **"How much, and what exactly do we get?"** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Discovery notes** card | After discovery at Crescent Tiles and Stone, the consultant has forty pages of notes. The managing director asks, so how much will it cost, and what exactly will we get? Notes cannot answer that. They must become a structured, numbered, classified and prioritised list, the requirement traceability matrix, which is used to estimate, design, test and finally accept the system. |
| 3 | **A testable statement of a business need**. Cards: Bad · Good · SAL-002 | A requirement is a testable statement of what the business needs the system to do. Compare these two. System should handle credit properly cannot be tested by anyone. The good version, numbered S A L zero zero two, says block sales order confirmation when the customer exceeds the credit limit, unless the sales manager approves. It is specific, testable, business-oriented, and uniquely numbered. |
| 4 | **The type decides who estimates and how it is tested**. Cards: Functional · Non-functional · Data · Reporting · Integration | There are five types of requirement. Functional describes what the system does, like quoting in square metres and delivering in boxes. Non-functional covers qualities like thirty users working at once. Data covers what must be migrated. Reporting covers outputs for management. Integration covers exchange with other systems. The type matters, because performance needs load tests, data needs reconciliation, and integrations need both systems to test. |
| 5 | **Customisation is the last answer, not the first**. Animated flow: Standard? ▸ A setting? ▸ Process change? ▸ Must with a case? ▸ Otherwise | Each requirement gets one classification, and you find it by walking the questions in order. Does the standard product already do it? Then it only needs training. Can a standard setting achieve it? Then it is configuration. Would the business accept a reasonable process change? Then document it in the to-be. Only if it is a must with a clear business case does it become a customisation. Anything else is deferred, with the reason logged. |
| 6 | Must, Should, Could, Won't (this time) | Prioritise with Moscow prioritisation. Must means you cannot go live without it, typically legal or core process needs, and should stay below about sixty percent of the effort. Should is important, but a workaround exists for a while. Could is nice to have. And won't, this time, is explicitly out of scope for this phase, but it is recorded, so nobody has to argue about it again. |
| 7 | Step 70%: **"Everything is a Must"** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **INT-001 B2B portal** card | In the role-play, the managing director says everything is a must, because they are paying for a complete system. Do not just agree, and do not lead with budget. Instead, test each item: could you go live and run for a month without it, with a workaround? For the B2B portal, ask for facts. About twenty orders a week are typed in by sales, so it becomes a won't for this phase, logged as a phase two candidate. The credit block really is a must, but check the standard credit warning first. |
| 8 | An extract from Crescent's RTM | Here is part of Crescent's matrix. Each row also records the source workshop, the fit or gap, solution notes, effort in days, an owner, a design reference and test case numbers. That is what makes it traceable in both directions: from a requirement forward to its design and tests, and from a test case back to the requirement it proves. |
| 9 | Step 112: **From 70% Must to a controlled scope** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **RTM baseline** card | Crescent's draft matrix had one hundred and twelve requirements, seventy percent marked must. Asking for a workaround for each must produced forty eight musts, thirty six shoulds, eighteen coulds and ten won'ts. Moving the portal integration to phase two saved fifteen percent of the budget and four weeks. One tip: baseline the matrix at sign-off. After that, every change goes through a formal change request, or scope will quietly grow. |
| 10 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Testable requirements · Classify standard-first · Challenge the Musts · Trace and baseline | To sum up. Write requirements that are specific, testable, business-oriented and numbered. Classify each one, checking standard and configuration before customisation. Use Moscow prioritisation and challenge excessive musts. And use the matrix to link requirements to design and tests, then baseline it. Now try the exercises in this lesson, and practise the Moscow role-play with Crescent's managing director. |
