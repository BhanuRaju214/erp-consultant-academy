# Video script: Why Businesses Implement ERP

ERP Fundamentals lesson video (/videos/erpf-why-businesses-implement-erp.mp4), shown at the top of the lesson. Built from the lesson content; Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Why Businesses Implement ERP**. Find the pain behind the project, and turn it into measurable objectives. | Welcome to lesson two. In this video you will learn why businesses really implement ERP, how to recognise the driver behind what a client says, and how to turn vague wishes into a business case with measurable objectives. |
| 2 | **Something hurts**. Cards: Late deliveries · Stock differences · Slow profit figures · Growth | Companies rarely buy an ERP because it is fashionable. They buy one because something hurts. Customers complain about late deliveries, the auditor finds stock differences, the owner waits three weeks for a profit figure, or the business has grown from one branch to five. Your first job as a consultant is to understand why this particular client is implementing ERP, because that reason shapes the scope, the priorities, and how success will be measured. |
| 3 | Hear the driver behind the symptom | Clients describe symptoms, not drivers, so learn to translate. We opened two new branches and nothing scales means growth. I don't know my real margin per product means visibility. Anyone can give any discount means control, solved with access rights and approvals. We type every order three times means efficiency. Tax filing takes a week means compliance. And we promise dates we cannot keep means customer service. Naming the driver tells you what success looks like. |
| 4 | **Tangible vs intangible**. Cards: Tangible: money or time · Intangible: real but hard to quantify | ERP benefits come in two kinds. Tangible benefits can be measured in money or time: lower inventory because data is reliable, fewer hours on data entry, a faster month end close, and faster cash collection because invoices go out the day goods ship. Intangible benefits are real but harder to quantify: better decisions from trustworthy data, a better customer experience, and less dependency on the one person who knows the spreadsheet. A good business case uses both. |
| 5 | **Every industry hurts differently**. Cards: Manufacturing · Retail · Distribution · Professional services · Construction · Interior design | Triggers also differ by industry. Manufacturers need bills of materials, work orders and production costing. Retailers need point of sale linked to central inventory. Distributors need fast order processing across warehouses and landed costs. Professional services need timesheets and project profitability. Construction needs project costing and progress billing, and interior design needs custom quotations and purchases from many vendors per project. So learn the industry before you propose a scope. |
| 6 | **Six parts of a business case**. Animated flow: Current situation ▸ SMART objectives ▸ Scope ▸ Costs ▸ Benefits ▸ Risks | A business case connects drivers to measurable outcomes. Start with the current situation and evidence, for example, order entry takes eighteen minutes on average. Then set SMART objectives: reduce order entry to under five minutes within six months of go-live. Define the scope, list the costs, from licences and implementation to training and internal time, then the benefits, and finally the risks and how you will mitigate them. |
| 7 | Step ★: **Three objectives become acceptance criteria** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **ERP objectives** card | Consider UrbanNest Interiors, a fictional interior design firm with forty staff. Designers quote in spreadsheets, buyers order furniture from a dozen vendors, and the owner learns a project lost money only after it is finished. Their objectives were three: see estimated versus actual cost per project at any time, convert an approved quotation into purchase orders without retyping, and invoice clients in milestones. These became the acceptance criteria, and anything that did not support them was deferred to a later phase. |
| 8 | **Turn wishes into objectives**. Cards: Missing data · Wasted hours · Success | Clients often say we want everything automated. That is a wish, not an objective, and it cannot be tested. Turn it into something measurable by asking three questions. What decision can you not make today because data is missing? Which task wastes the most hours each week? And what would make this project a success in the owner's eyes twelve months from now? The answers give you priorities you can actually deliver and prove. |
| 9 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Pain drives ERP · Two kinds of benefit · Know the industry · Objectives are the yardstick | To recap. ERP projects are driven by business pain: growth, visibility, control, efficiency and compliance. Benefits are both tangible and intangible. Drivers differ by industry, so learn the industry first. And a business case links clear objectives to costs, benefits and risks, which then become the yardstick for scope decisions and acceptance. Now open the lesson and try the exercises: match each client statement to its driver, and pick the SMART objective for UrbanNest. |
