# Video script: Implementation Costs, Benefits, Risks and Success Factors

ERP Fundamentals lesson video (/videos/erpf-costs-benefits-risks-success-factors.mp4), shown at the top of the lesson. Built from the lesson content; Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Implementation Costs, Benefits, Risks and Success Factors**. Draw the full cost picture, measure the benefits, and manage the risks. | Welcome to lesson eleven of E R P Fundamentals. In this video you will learn how to see the full cost of an E R P project, how to measure its benefits, which risks to watch for, and the factors that really decide success. |
| 2 | Step 45%: **The costs nobody drew** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **ERP budget · Tidewater** card | Tidewater Components, an electronics assembler with two hundred staff, approved its E R P budget based only on the software subscription and consultant fees. Six months in, the hidden costs appeared: staff overtime for testing, a new fleet of barcode scanners, a temporary team to clean data, and two extra months of the old system's licence. The project went forty five percent over budget. Nobody was dishonest; the full cost picture was simply never drawn. |
| 3 | The costs budgets miss | Every budget includes software and implementation services. What gets forgotten are data migration, with its cleansing, mapping and reconciliation; hardware like scanners and label printers; the time of your own staff in workshops and testing, plus backfill for their daily jobs; parallel running of the old system; and future upgrades. Hosting, training and post go-live support are sometimes missed too. A full total cost of ownership model lists all of them up front. |
| 4 | Measure benefits from a baseline | Benefits must be measured, not hoped for. Each one needs a key performance indicator, a baseline and a target. For example, order processing from eighteen minutes to five, month end close from ten days to four, days sales outstanding from sixty two to forty eight, and on time delivery from eighty two to ninety five percent. Most important, each benefit must be owned by a business manager and measured after go-live, otherwise it is only a hope. |
| 5 | Spot the warning signs early | Each major risk has an early warning sign. Scope creep shows up as requirements still growing after sign-off; control it with formal change requests. Poor data shows up as late duplicates; start cleansing in discovery and run several trial loads. Low adoption shows up as shadow spreadsheets. Over-customization, weak sponsorship and unrealistic timelines each have their signs too. Key person dependency and unclear integration specifications complete the list. |
| 6 | **People, data and decisions**. Cards: Committed sponsor · Clear objectives · Dedicated key users · Standard first · Clean data · Thorough testing · Change management · Phased approach · Communication · Post-go-live owner | There are ten critical success factors, and notice how few of them are about software. A committed sponsor who decides on time. Clear, measurable objectives and controlled scope. Dedicated key users. A standard first approach. Clean data owned by the business. Thorough testing with real scenarios. Change management and training, because people, not software, decide adoption. A phased approach where sensible, strong communication with decision logs, and someone who owns the system after go-live. |
| 7 | Phased vs big bang | How should you go live? A big bang switches all modules and all sites on one day. It is faster but riskier. A phased approach starts with core processes, such as finance, sales and stock, then adds manufacturing and other sites in later waves. It reduces risk, but needs temporary interfaces between the old and new systems. Choose deliberately. |
| 8 | Step Tip: **Readiness, not dates** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Phase 1 results** card | A classic mistake is to compress user testing three weeks before go-live just to keep the date. That only moves defects into production. Tidewater reset its project with a full cost model, a fifteen percent contingency and a phased plan. Each phase had go-live criteria: all critical tests passed, opening balances reconciled, and every user trained. Phase one went live a month late, but with no disruption, and inventory accuracy rose from eighty eight to ninety eight percent within three months. |
| 9 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Budget the full cost · Own the benefits · Watch the top risks · Go live on readiness | To sum up. Budget the full cost, including data, hardware, internal time, parallel running and upgrades. Give every benefit a baseline, a K P I and a named owner. Watch the top risks: scope creep, bad data, low adoption, over-customization and weak sponsorship. Success depends more on people, data and decisions than on software features, so go live on readiness, not on a date. Now try the exercises in this lesson, starting with matching Tidewater's forgotten costs to their categories. |
