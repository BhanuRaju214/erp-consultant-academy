# Video script: Problems with Disconnected Applications

ERP Fundamentals lesson video (/videos/erpf-problems-with-disconnected-applications.mp4), shown at the top of the lesson. Built from the lesson content; Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Problems with Disconnected Applications**. Islands of data, connected only by people re-typing information. | Welcome to lesson three. In this video you will learn why a business running on separate tools struggles, the seven core problems you will document during discovery, and how to put a number on each one. |
| 2 | **Five tools, five versions of the truth**. Cards: Online store · Order spreadsheet · CRM tool · Warehouse sheet · Accounting package · Result | Most small and medium businesses grow organically. They start with spreadsheets, add an accounting package, then an online store and a separate CRM. Each tool solves one problem well, but together they become islands of data. Orders are exported weekly, re-typed into accounting, and stock levels sit in a sheet only the storekeeper can see. The result is typos, missed orders, delays, and no single trusted view of the business. |
| 3 | **Double entry, no truth, stale data**. Cards: 1. Double entry · 2. No single source of truth · 3. Delayed information | Let's look at the core problems. First, double entry: the same customer or order is typed into several systems, and a wrong digit in a price travels silently until a customer complains. Second, no single source of truth. If the CRM says a customer owes ten thousand, accounting says twelve thousand five hundred, and the sales sheet says nine thousand eight hundred, meetings become arguments about whose numbers to trust. Third, delayed information: with weekly exports, managers sell stock that was already sold. |
| 4 | **Spreadsheets are flexible but fragile**. Cards: Overwritten formulas · Many versions · No control, no audit trail · One person's head | Fourth, spreadsheet risk. Spreadsheets are flexible, which is why everyone loves them, but they are fragile. Formulas get overwritten by accident. Multiple versions circulate by email, with names like stock version seven final new. There is no access control and no audit trail, so anyone can change a past figure without a trace. And the knowledge of how it all works lives in one person's head. |
| 5 | **Weak controls, poor reporting, slow close**. Cards: 5. Weak controls · 6. Poor reporting · 7. Reconciliation | Fifth, weak controls. Without integrated workflows, approvals happen by email or not at all. A purchase skips the budget check, a discount is given without a manager, or goods ship to a customer who is over the credit limit. Sixth, poor reporting: combining several tools takes days, and the result cannot be drilled down to the source transaction. Seventh, reconciliation overhead: finance spends days at month end matching figures across systems. |
| 6 | Put a number on every problem | During discovery, put a number on each problem. For double entry, multiply minutes per transaction by transactions per month by hourly cost. For errors, count credit notes and returns caused by wrong data. For delays, count stock outs or overstock incidents. For reconciliation, measure days to close the books. Numbers make the business case convincing, and they give you a baseline to prove value after go-live. |
| 7 | Step ★: **What discovery found** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Discovery findings** card | Coastal Supplies Trading, a fictional distributor, keyed each order twice, about nine extra minutes on fourteen hundred orders a month. Nine times fourteen hundred is twelve thousand six hundred minutes, roughly two hundred and ten hours a month, more than one full time person. Their three warehouse sheets disagreed with the accounting stock value by about six percent at every audit, and over limit customers still got goods. The ERP proposal targeted exactly these points. |
| 8 | **Integration is not the same as ERP**. Cards: Gluing tools together · ERP core on one database | A common mistake is to think that gluing separate tools together with integrations gives you the same result as ERP. It can work, but each connector must be built, monitored and maintained, and master data still lives in several places. ERP reduces the number of integrations by putting core processes on one database. Integrations are then reserved for genuinely external systems such as banks, couriers, marketplaces and government portals. |
| 9 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Islands of data · Trust is the first casualty · Quantify everything · Fewer, not zero, integrations | To recap. Disconnected tools cause double entry, conflicting numbers, delays, weak controls and slow reporting. No single source of truth is the most damaging problem, because people stop trusting data. Spreadsheets lack access control, audit trail and version control. Quantify each problem in discovery, and remember that ERP reduces, but does not eliminate, integrations. Now open the lesson and try the exercises: match each problem to its measure, and calculate Coastal Supplies' lost hours yourself. |
