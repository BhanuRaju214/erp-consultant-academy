# Video script: CRM vs ERP vs Accounting Software vs Standalone Apps

ERP Fundamentals lesson video (/videos/erpf-crm-vs-erp-vs-accounting.mp4), shown at the top of the lesson. Built from the lesson content; Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **CRM vs ERP vs Accounting Software vs Standalone Apps**. Know what each category does, where its boundaries lie, and what it costs to connect them. | Welcome to lesson five. In this video you will learn the four categories of business software, when each one is the right answer, and the hidden cost behind the phrase, can't we just connect what we have. |
| 2 | Step ?: **“Why do I need ERP?”** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Current software** card | Clients often say, we already have software. PeakForm Retail, a fictional chain of six sports goods stores, has a popular accounting package, a cloud CRM and a point of sale system. The owner asks, why do I need ERP, can't I just connect what I have? It is a fair question. To answer it honestly, you must understand what each category of software does and where its boundaries lie. |
| 3 | **Four categories of business software**. Cards: Accounting software · CRM · Standalone apps · ERP | Accounting software records financial transactions: invoices, bills, payments, ledgers and tax returns. Some have basic inventory, but usually no warehouses, lots or manufacturing. CRM manages the front office: leads, pipeline and activities. It knows the customer very well but knows nothing about stock or costs. Standalone applications do one job, often excellently. And ERP covers the core operations of the whole business on one database. |
| 4 | How the categories compare | Side by side, the differences are clear. Accounting serves finance and covers record to report. CRM serves sales and marketing and covers lead to opportunity. ERP serves all departments and covers end to end processes like Order to Cash and Procure to Pay, with full inventory. The trade off is effort: ERP takes more effort to implement, but needs far fewer integrations because the core is built in. Standalone apps serve one team, need little effort, but need high integration. |
| 5 | **Match the software to the business**. Cards: Accounting only · CRM + accounting · Best-of-breed stack · ERP | So when is each the right answer? Accounting alone suits a small service business with few transactions and no stock. CRM plus accounting suits a sales driven business with simple invoicing. A best of breed stack suits specialised needs where one tool is far superior, but it needs integration investment and an owner for each connector. Choose ERP when processes cross departments, inventory matters, there are several sites, or management needs consolidated real time reporting. Modular ERPs like Odoo let a company start small and add apps later. |
| 6 | The hidden cost of “just connect them” | The common mistake is to assume connecting tools is a one time job. First you build the connector, mapping fields between systems with different data models. Then master data lives in two places and needs syncing rules. If the sync fails at two in the morning, who notices? Every vendor upgrades on its own schedule and may break the connector. And cross functional reports still need consolidation. These costs last for the life of the system. |
| 7 | **ERP core, one tool kept**. Animated flow: POS ▸ Inventory ▸ Purchase ▸ Accounting ▸ CRM data ▸ Email marketing | For PeakForm, the consultant mapped the flows. Store sales reached accounting through a nightly file, stock transfers between stores were on paper, and the CRM had loyalty customers but no purchase history. The recommendation was an ERP covering point of sale, inventory, purchase and accounting on one database, with the CRM data migrated in. The cloud CRM was retired, removing one subscription and one connector. The email marketing tool stayed, because it had no inventory or financial impact, so integration was simple and low risk. |
| 8 | **Ask about boundaries, not brands**. Cards: Where are the boundaries? · What does connecting cost? | Here is the consultant's tip. The question is never which tool is the best. The question is where the boundaries are, and what it costs to connect them. Follow the processes that cross departments, like order to stock to invoice, and put those on one database. Then keep standalone tools only where they add real value and have low financial or stock impact. |
| 9 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: Know the categories · Cross-department = ERP · Connectors cost for life · Start small, keep what works | To recap. Accounting software records finances, CRM manages customer relationships, point apps do one job, and ERP integrates core operations. The more processes cross departments, the stronger the case for ERP. Connecting separate tools has ongoing costs for mapping, syncing, monitoring and upgrades. And modular ERPs let a company start small and keep useful standalone tools. Now open the lesson and try the exercises: walk through the decision tree and match each category to the job it does best. |
