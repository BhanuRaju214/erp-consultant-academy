# Video script: What is ERP?

ERP Fundamentals lesson video (/videos/erpf-what-is-erp.mp4), shown at the top of the lesson. Built from the lesson content; Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **What is ERP?**. One database and one set of master data, so every department works on the same information in real time. | Welcome to ERP Fundamentals, lesson one. In this video you will learn what an ERP system really is, why integration is the heart of it, which resources it manages, and where it came from. Most importantly, you will learn what ERP is not. |
| 2 | **One sales order, five hand-offs**. Cards: Sales · Stock check · Warehouse · Purchasing · Accounts · Result | Meet Brightline Furniture, a fictional manufacturer of office chairs with one hundred and twenty employees. Sales takes an order by email, types it into a spreadsheet, and phones the warehouse to ask if stock exists. The warehouse checks a different spreadsheet, purchasing buys fabric from a printed list, and accounts raise the invoice two weeks later, when someone remembers. Every hand-off depends on a person remembering to pass information along, and that is exactly the problem ERP was created to solve. |
| 3 | **Three words that define ERP**. Cards: Integrated · Shared data · Process-oriented | So what is ERP? Enterprise Resource Planning is an integrated suite of business applications that share one database and one set of master data. Three words capture it. Integrated, because a transaction in one area automatically updates the others. Shared data, because a customer, product or vendor is created once and used everywhere. And process oriented, because the system follows complete business flows such as Order to Cash or Procure to Pay, rather than isolated tasks. |
| 4 | **Five resources an ERP manages**. Cards: Money · Materials · People · Capacity · Customers | The word resource matters. An ERP plans and tracks five kinds of resources. Money, such as cash, receivables and budgets, lives in accounting. Materials live in inventory, purchase and manufacturing. People, with their timesheets and payroll, live in HR. Capacity means machines, work centres and vehicles. And customers and relationships live in CRM and sales. Because all five share one database, a decision about one resource can immediately consider the others. |
| 5 | **From MRP to cloud ERP**. Animated flow: MRP ▸ MRP II ▸ ERP ▸ Cloud ERP | ERP did not appear overnight. In the nineteen sixties and seventies, Material Requirements Planning, or MRP, calculated which raw materials to buy from a production plan. In the eighties, MRP two added capacity planning, shop floor control and costing. In the nineties, ERP extended the idea to finance, HR, sales and distribution in one system. Since then, cloud ERP has added web access, e-commerce, mobile apps and software as a service. Odoo is a modular example, with separate apps that all share one database. |
| 6 | **One sales order flows through the business**. Animated flow: Order confirmed ▸ Stock reserved ▸ Delivery order ▸ Goods shipped ▸ Invoice ▸ Journal entry | Here is integration in action. The salesperson confirms the order, and the ERP reserves stock automatically. If stock is short, it suggests a purchase. The warehouse sees the delivery to prepare without being told, and validating it reduces stock. Accounts create the invoice from the order, with price, taxes and customer already filled in, and posting it records the journal entry. One transaction, six departments updated, and no re-typing. |
| 7 | What changed after go-live | After go-live, Brightline's salesperson sees available stock right on the quotation line. The warehouse automatically sees deliveries, purchasing gets replenishment suggestions for short fabric, and accounts invoice with one click. The month end close dropped from eight days to three. The four spreadsheets that needed reconciling are gone, and invoices now go out on delivery instead of two weeks later. That is the measurable value of shared data. |
| 8 | **What ERP is not**. Cards: Not just accounting · Not a process fix · Not install and forget | As a consultant, you will hear three misconceptions again and again. First, that ERP is just accounting software. Accounting is only one module; ERP is the platform for every department. Second, that ERP will fix a poor process. It will not. Automating a bad process only produces bad results faster, so improve the process first. Third, that ERP is install and forget. Plan for configuration, data, training and ongoing ownership. |
| 9 | **Key takeaways**. Cards: One database · Integration · From MRP to ERP · People and process | To recap. ERP means integrated applications on one database with shared master data. Integration means one transaction updates every affected department automatically, supporting end to end processes like Order to Cash and Procure to Pay. ERP grew from MRP to cover finance, people, customers and capacity. And technology alone does not fix a business; process and people matter just as much. Now open the lesson and try the exercises: map the hand-offs in a business you know, and test yourself with the questions. |
