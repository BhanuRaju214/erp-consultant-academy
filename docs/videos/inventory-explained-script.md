# Video script: Inventory Management, explained

Animated explainer for the Inventory Management business process (video /videos/inventory-explained.mp4 in the portal). Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Inventory Management**. Where every unit is, how it got there, and what it is worth. | Welcome. In this video you will learn the Inventory Management process: how stock is created, received, moved, delivered, counted and valued, and how Odoo records every single movement. |
| 2 | **What is Inventory Management?**. Cards: Know where it is · Promise reliably · Value it correctly | Inventory management covers the whole life of stock inside the business. The goal is to keep the right quantity of each item in the right place at the right time, with an auditable record of every movement. Then sales can promise reliably, purchasing buys only what is needed, and the inventory value on the balance sheet matches the physical stock. |
| 3 | **Who takes part?**. Cards: Master Data Officer · Warehouse Manager · Storekeeper · Inventory Controller · Inventory Planner · Cost Accountant · Store Manager | Several roles take part. The master data officer creates the items. The warehouse manager designs locations and routes. Storekeepers receive, move and ship goods. The inventory controller runs the counts, the planner sets reordering rules, and the cost accountant makes sure the stock value matches the general ledger. |
| 4 | **Nine steps in the life of stock**. Animated flow: Create item ▸ Warehouses ▸ Opening stock ▸ Receive ▸ Transfer ▸ Reserve ▸ Deliver ▸ Count ▸ Value | Here is the big picture: nine steps. Create the item, set up warehouses and locations, load opening stock, receive goods, transfer them, reserve them for orders, deliver them, count them, and value the inventory. In Odoo every movement is a stock move from one location to another, and with automated valuation it posts to accounting. |
| 5 | Step 1–2: **Create the item, set up the warehouse** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Product** card | Steps one and two. Every stocked item needs a product record: type, unit of measure, barcode, and a product category that carries the costing method and the stock accounts. UrbanThread Retail sets its denim jackets to average cost with automated valuation. Then the warehouses, locations and routes are set up. No transactions yet, but wrong settings here cause wrong figures later. |
| 6 | Step 3: **Load opening stock** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Opening stock** card | Step three: opening stock. At go-live, the quantities on hand are counted and loaded per product, location and lot. UrbanThread counts five hundred jackets at a cost of forty, an opening value of twenty thousand. The value posts against a clearing account that finance ties back to the legacy balance sheet. |
| 7 | Step 4: **Receive stock** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Receipt** card | Step four. Goods from vendors are checked against the expected receipt, counted and put away. One hundred jackets arrive at forty six each. Because UrbanThread uses average cost, the new average becomes forty one. Lots, serial numbers and expiry dates are recorded at this point, so each item stays traceable. |
| 8 | Step 5: **Internal and inter-warehouse transfers** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Transfer to Store North** card | Step five. Stock moves between bins, or from the central warehouse to a store. Store North requests one hundred and twenty jackets, and the resupply route creates a delivery from the warehouse and a receipt at the store. The total company value is unchanged, so there is no journal entry. |
| 9 | Step 6–7: **Reserve and deliver** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Delivery** card | Steps six and seven. When an order needs stock, specific units are reserved, so two orders never promise the same jackets. An online order for thirty jackets lowers the free to use quantity from four hundred and eighty to four hundred and fifty, while on hand stays the same. When the delivery is validated, on hand goes down and the cost of goods sold is recognised when the order is invoiced. |
| 10 | Step 8: **Count the stock** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Cycle count · Zone A** card | Step eight: counting. Stock is counted regularly, often by cycle counting a portion of locations every week. The system shows four hundred and fifty jackets, but the team counts four hundred and forty seven. The controller investigates, finds no error, and applies the adjustment with the reason shrinkage. Every adjustment changes profit, so it needs approval. |
| 11 | Step 9: **Value inventory, reconcile the ledger** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Valuation vs ledger** card | Step nine. At each period end, finance confirms that the stock valuation report equals the inventory balance in the general ledger. UrbanThread has five hundred and twenty seven jackets at forty one, twenty one thousand six hundred and seven, and the ledger shows exactly the same. Slow moving items are reviewed for write-down. |
| 12 | **The journal entries behind inventory**: 1. Receipt 100 at 46 · 2. Delivery 50, then invoice · 3. Count shortage 3 units | Let's follow the money. A receipt of one hundred jackets at forty six debits stock valuation four thousand six hundred. A delivery of fifty at the average of forty one credits stock valuation two thousand and fifty, and the customer invoice moves it to cost of goods sold. A count shortage of three units costs one hundred and twenty three. Internal transfers post nothing at all. |
| 13 | **Common risks, and how Odoo controls them**. Cards: Negative stock · Shrinkage and theft · Expired or obsolete stock · Valuation does not match ledger | What can go wrong? Negative stock: train operators to validate receipts before deliveries. Shrinkage: count high value items often and approve adjustments above a threshold. Expired stock: use expiration dates and first expiry, first out. And a valuation that does not match the ledger: forbid manual entries on stock accounts and reconcile every month. |
| 14 | **Every unit, every move, every value**. Animated flow: Item ▸ Warehouse ▸ Opening ▸ Receipt ▸ Transfer ▸ Reserve ▸ Delivery ▸ Count ▸ Valuation | To recap: item, warehouse, opening stock, receipt, transfer, reservation, delivery, count and valuation. Every unit, every move, every value. Next, open the Odoo Inventory course and practise these operations yourself with the click-through simulators. |
