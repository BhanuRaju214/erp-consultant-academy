# Video script: Procure to Pay, explained

Animated explainer for the Procure to Pay business process (video /videos/p2p-explained.mp4 in the portal). Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Procure to Pay**. How a business need becomes a paid vendor bill, and how Odoo controls every step. | Welcome. In this video you will learn the Procure to Pay process: how a business need becomes a purchase order, a receipt, a vendor bill and finally a payment, and how Odoo controls every single step. |
| 2 | **What is Procure to Pay?**. Cards: Buy right · Pay only what is due · Pay on time | Procure to Pay, or P2P, is the complete purchasing cycle. It starts when someone identifies a need and ends when the vendor is paid. The goal: buy the right goods at the right price from approved vendors, pay only for what was ordered and received, and pay on time to protect the relationship. |
| 3 | **Who takes part?**. Cards: Requester · Budget Owner · Buyer · Purchasing Manager · Storekeeper · Quality Inspector · Accounts Payable · Treasury | Many people take part. The requester raises the need and the budget owner approves it. The buyer selects the vendor and issues the purchase order, and the purchasing manager approves large orders. The storekeeper and quality inspector receive the goods. Accounts payable records the bill, and treasury pays the vendor. |
| 4 | **Seven steps from need to payment**. Animated flow: Requisition ▸ Select vendor ▸ RFQ ▸ Purchase order ▸ Receive ▸ Vendor bill ▸ Payment | Here is the big picture: seven steps. Raise the requisition, select the vendor, send the request for quotation, confirm the purchase order, receive the goods, record the vendor bill with the three way match, and pay the vendor. In Odoo these steps run across Purchase, Inventory and Accounting, all linked in one database. |
| 5 | Step 1–2: **Raise the need, select the vendor** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Vendor comparison** card | Steps one and two. Site engineer Rahul at Stonebridge Builders needs two hundred bags of cement by the twelfth of June. His request is approved within the project budget. Buyer Meera compares three vendors. BuildMart is cheapest but cannot deliver in time, so she selects CemCo at thirty four a bag. |
| 6 | Step 3: **Send the Request for Quotation** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Request for Quotation** card | Step three: the request for quotation. The buyer lists the items, quantities, required date and delivery address, and emails it to the vendor. When the vendor replies, the prices and lead times are entered on the R F Q lines. Requests that are not selected are cancelled. |
| 7 | Step 4: **Approve and confirm the Purchase Order** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Purchase Order · CemCo** card | Step four. The selected R F Q is confirmed into a purchase order, a legal commitment to buy. Stonebridge's cement order totals seven thousand eight hundred and twenty, above the approval threshold of five thousand, so it waits in To Approve until the purchasing manager approves it. Odoo then creates the expected receipt. Still no journal entry. |
| 8 | Step 5: **Receive the goods** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Receipt WH/IN/00087** card | Step five. When the truck arrives, the storekeeper compares the delivery note with the order, counts the goods and checks for damage. CemCo delivers only one hundred and ninety bags, so Odoo creates a backorder for the remaining ten. Validating the receipt increases stock on hand and, with automated valuation, posts the stock entry. |
| 9 | Step 6: **Record the bill, perform the 3-way match** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Vendor Bill · CemCo** card | Step six: the vendor bill. Accounts payable checks it against the purchase order for price, and against the receipt for quantity. This is the three way match. CemCo bills thirty five a bag instead of the agreed thirty four, so Joseph puts the bill on hold until a corrected bill arrives. The match prevents an overpayment of two hundred plus tax. |
| 10 | Step 7: **Pay the vendor** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Vendor Bill · CemCo** card | Step seven. Approved bills are paid according to their due dates, often in a weekly payment run. CemCo's corrected bill of seven thousand eight hundred and twenty is paid in the Friday batch. When the bank statement arrives, the line is matched with the payment, and the cycle is closed. |
| 11 | **The journal entries behind P2P**: 1. Receipt validated · 2. Vendor bill posted · 3. Payment and bank | Let's follow the money. The receipt debits stock valuation five thousand against the stock input account. The bill clears stock input, adds seven hundred and fifty of input tax, and credits payables five thousand seven hundred and fifty. The payment clears payables, and the bank reconciliation moves the amount out of the bank. At month end, the stock input balance shows goods received but not yet billed. |
| 12 | **Common risks, and how Odoo controls them**. Cards: Buying without a PO · Partial or excess delivery · Bill price differs from PO · Duplicate bill or changed bank account | What can go wrong? Buying without a purchase order: enforce no P O, no pay. Partial deliveries: use backorders and bill only received quantities. A bill price that differs from the order: the three way match stops it. And duplicate bills or a fraudulent change of bank account: Odoo warns on duplicate vendor references, and bank changes are verified by call-back. |
| 13 | **One process, one database**. Animated flow: Need ▸ Vendor ▸ RFQ ▸ Purchase order ▸ Receipt ▸ Vendor bill ▸ Payment | To recap: need, vendor, request for quotation, purchase order, receipt, vendor bill and payment. Pay only for what was ordered and received. Next, open the Odoo Purchase course and practise the flow yourself with the click-through simulators. |
