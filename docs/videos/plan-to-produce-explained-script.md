# Video script: Plan to Produce, explained

Animated explainer for the Plan to Produce business process (video /videos/plan-to-produce-explained.mp4 in the portal). Odoo colours, nexusEPM logo, narration by Microsoft neural voice Neerja (Indian English) with captions.

| # | On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title card: **Plan to Produce**. How raw materials, people and machines become finished goods, at a known cost. | Welcome. In this video you will learn the Plan to Produce process: how a product is defined, planned, made and costed, and how Odoo Manufacturing supports every step. |
| 2 | **What is Plan to Produce?**. Cards: Define the product · Plan and produce · Know the cost | Plan to Produce is the manufacturing cycle. It defines what a product is made of and how it is made, plans and releases production orders, issues the materials, runs the operations and receives the finished goods. The goal is to produce on time, at the expected quality and at the lowest cost, and to give finance a reliable product cost. |
| 3 | **Who takes part?**. Cards: Product Engineer · Production Planner · Production Supervisor · Machine Operator · Storekeeper · Quality Inspector · Cost Accountant | Many people take part. The product engineer defines the bill of materials and operations. The planner creates the manufacturing orders, and the supervisor releases them to the shop floor. Operators run the work orders, the storekeeper issues components and stores the finished goods, quality inspects, and the cost accountant analyses the cost. |
| 4 | **Seven steps from recipe to cost**. Animated flow: Bill of Materials ▸ Work centers ▸ Manufacturing order ▸ Issue materials ▸ Produce ▸ Finished goods ▸ Cost analysis | Here is the big picture: seven steps. Define the bill of materials, define the work centers and operations, create the manufacturing order, issue the materials, execute the operations, receive the finished goods, and analyse the cost. Manufacturing sits on top of Inventory: components go into production and finished goods come out. |
| 5 | Step 1–2: **Define the BoM and the work centers** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **BoM · Heritage Dining Table** card | Steps one and two. The bill of materials lists every component needed to make one unit. For Oakridge Furniture's Heritage Dining Table, the materials cost one hundred and ninety four. The operations, cutting, assembly and finishing, run at work centers with an hourly cost, and add seventy two fifty. So the expected cost of one table is two hundred and sixty six fifty. |
| 6 | Step 3: **Plan and create the manufacturing order** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **MO WH/MO/00118** card | Step three. A hotel chain orders twenty tables, and the make to order route creates the manufacturing order automatically. The planner checks availability: only sixty legs against eighty needed. A reordering rule creates a purchase order for the legs, and the order is confirmed with a start date after they arrive. Confirmation reserves components, but posts no journal entry. |
| 7 | Step 4: **Issue materials to production** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Pick Components** card | Step four. Components are picked from the store and delivered to the production line. Oakridge uses two step manufacturing, so the storekeeper validates the pick: twenty tabletops, eighty legs, three hundred and twenty screws and ten litres of varnish. This is only a location change, so there is no journal entry yet. |
| 8 | Step 5: **Execute production operations** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Work orders** card | Step five. Operators perform the operations in sequence, recording start and stop times and any scrap. At Oakridge, assembly takes eighteen hours against fifteen planned, and one tabletop cracks and is scrapped. The actual conversion cost is one thousand five hundred and seventy, against one thousand four hundred and fifty expected. |
| 9 | Step 6: **Receive finished goods into stock** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **MO WH/MO/00118** card | Step six. When production is complete, the produced quantity is declared and the finished goods move into stock, ready for sale. Components are consumed, and the twenty tables enter stock at five thousand four hundred and fifty, an actual unit cost of two hundred and seventy two fifty. |
| 10 | Step 7: **Analyse production cost** (who, Odoo menu, impact) with an animated **Cost analysis** card | Step seven. The actual cost of the order is compared with the expected cost. Each table cost six more than planned, mostly in assembly. Labour efficiency was ninety one point eight percent, and the scrap rate four point eight percent. With a selling price of five hundred and twenty, the margin is still forty seven point six percent, and the team plans training and a saw fixture. |
| 11 | **The journal entries behind production**: 1. Components consumed · 2. Finished goods produced · 3. Scrap of a component | Let's follow the money. Consuming the components moves three thousand eight hundred and eighty from raw materials into work in progress. Producing the tables moves five thousand four hundred and fifty from work in progress into finished goods. The difference, a credit of one thousand five hundred and seventy, is labour and overhead absorbed into product cost. A scrapped component is booked as a loss. |
| 12 | **Common risks, and how Odoo controls them**. Cards: Component shortage · Inaccurate BoM · Time not recorded · Wrong product cost | What can go wrong? Component shortages: check availability before confirming and use reordering rules. An inaccurate bill of materials: compare actual and expected consumption. Operators not recording time: review work orders with zero duration. And a wrong product cost: maintain realistic work center costs and review the cost analysis every month. |
| 13 | **From recipe to real cost**. Animated flow: BoM ▸ Work centers ▸ MO ▸ Components ▸ Work orders ▸ Finished goods ▸ Cost | To recap: bill of materials, work centers, manufacturing order, components, work orders, finished goods and cost.
